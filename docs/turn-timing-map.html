Turn Timing Map

This page follows one caller turn: how the agent decides the caller has finished, when the reply starts, and when the caller can still cut the reply off. Times count from the caller's last word. Phase and function names match turn_taking.py.

Deciding the caller has finished

  • A question means a ? in the last 60 characters, or a hand-off phrase such as “what do you think” or “go ahead”.
  • A complete statement ends in ., ! or ? and has at least 3 words. Whisper usually punctuates, so most statements qualify.
  • Anything else is held: no end punctuation, fewer than 3 words, or a trailing word like “and”, “the” or “because”.
  • Speech between 400 and 800ms is transcribed separately and joined onto the pending text.

The three caller phases

The reply, and when the caller can cut it off

  • What sets it off: 250ms of speech (BARGE_IN_MS) while the agent is talking: writing a reply, sending it, or still playing it.
  • What it does: cancels the reply, then sends Twilio clear, which drops the audio queued for the phone. The caller hears about 300ms of the agent after starting to talk; before 1 Nov it was 6–17s.
  • Shorter sounds don't stop it: a cough or click under 250ms is ignored, and its transcript gets no reply.
  • Acknowledgements resume the reply: an “mm-hmm” or “okay” is longer than 250ms, so it stops the agent. About 1s later, once its transcript shows it was only an acknowledgement, the reply picks up from the start of the sentence the caller was hearing, or the clause, for a long sentence. A sound with no words in it (a breath, a laugh) does the same, about 0.8s after it ends.
  • Claude's history keeps what the caller heard: after a real interruption, only the sentences that finished playing stay in the history.
  • During the greeting barge-in is off until the greeting has finished playing.

How long a mid-sentence pause can be

If the caller pauses mid-thought, a reply may start during the pause. When they resume before its first audio arrives, barge-in cancels it after 250ms of their speech, and nobody hears it. So a pause is safe while it's shorter than the time from the caller stopping to the reply's first audio: the reply start (about 0.55s after a question, 0.8s after a statement) plus Claude's first sentence and TTS. On 31 Oct that came to about 2.4s at the median, and Claude's spread makes the edge fuzzy.

Known gaps

  • An “mm-hmm” costs about a second. The agent stops, waits for the transcript, then repeats the sentence or clause that was playing.
  • A “yes” said mid-reply resumes the reply. If the agent asked a question and kept talking, that answer goes unanswered.
  • A “Hello?” during the greeting starts a second reply, whose audio queues behind the greeting.
  • One STT instance is shared by every call, so two calls at once would mix their VAD state.
